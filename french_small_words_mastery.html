1. The complete map

French small words are not one category. The words you asked about belong to several grammar families.

FamilyWordsMain functionExample
Definite articlesle, la, l’, lesthe; known/specific/general nounle français = French / the French language
Indefinite articlesun, une, desa/an/some; non-specific countable nounune question = a question
Partitive articlesdu, de la, de l’, dessome/any; amount not counteddu café = coffee / some coffee
Contractions with àau, auxà + le / à + lesau parc = to/at the park
Contractions with dedu, desde + le / de + lesdu parc = from/of the park
Simple prepositionsà, de, en, dans, chez, avec, sans, pour, par, sur...relationship: place, time, cause, direction, meanschez moi = at my place
Quantity expressionsbeaucoup de, trop de, assez de, un peu de...amount + de/d’beaucoup d’amis = many friends
Possessive determinersmon, ma, mes, ton, ta, tes, son...my/your/his/her/our/theirmes livres = my books
Demonstrative determinersce, cet, cette, cesthis/that/these/thosecet homme = this/that man
Pronounsy, enreplace à/de phrases or quantitiesJ’y vais. J’en veux.
Most important idea: au, aux, du, des are not random. They are forced contractions: à + le = au, à + les = aux, de + le = du, de + les = des. But du, de la, de l’, des can also be partitive articles meaning “some.” Context decides.

7. Mistake clinic

Classic mistakes

  • à le → always au when le is an article.
  • de les → always des when les is an article.
  • Negative food/quantity: Je ne veux pas de café, not usually pas du café.
  • Quantities use de/d’: beaucoup de travail, trop d’eau.
  • Countries: en France, au Canada, aux États-Unis.
  • People’s homes/professional places: chez moi, chez le médecin.
  • Do not contract before object pronouns: Je commence à le faire, not au faire.
  • y usually replaces an à place/thing, not a person.
  • en replaces de phrases or quantities: J’en ai deux.
